Why small teams go looking for an alternative

Apollo's free tier gets a lot of people through the door. The trouble starts when a small team tries to use it seriously. Three things come up again and again:

  • Per-seat pricing punishes small teams. Five reps on the Professional plan is $395 a month billed annually, or $495 month-to-month, before anyone reveals a phone number. The Organization plan has a three-seat minimum.
  • Credits are the real bill. A verified email costs one credit, a mobile number costs eight, and enrichment runs one to eight per record. Teams routinely find their budget disappearing into the credit meter rather than the seat fee.
  • It's a lot of product. Sequences, a dialer, meeting scheduling, intent data, workflows. For a team without a sales-ops person, that breadth is setup time and training, not value.

None of that makes Apollo a bad tool. It makes it a tool sized for a different team.

Where Apollo wins

Honest comparisons rank better and convert better, so here's the case for Apollo.

Volume. Apollo's database is enormous and its credit allowances on paid plans run into the thousands. If your motion is high-volume outbound, where a rep touches hundreds of new contacts a week, Zelvarix's reveal limits will feel tight even with top-ups.

Everything in one place. Sequences, calling, scheduling, and a lightweight CRM under one login. Teams that have the discipline and the ops support to run all of it get real leverage from having it unified.

A free tier that stays free. For a solo founder who sends twenty emails a month, Apollo's free plan may be all they ever need.

How to decide

Ask two questions about your team.

Is our problem "not enough contacts" or "not enough replies"? Most small teams have plenty of names and get ignored because the first message says nothing. That's a Zelvarix problem. If you have great messaging and need ten times more names, that's an Apollo problem.

Who's going to run the tool? If the answer is "the reps, in the gaps between calls," pick the one that doesn't need a manual. If you have someone whose job is sales operations, Apollo's breadth becomes usable.

Plenty of teams use both: Apollo or a similar tool for volume lists, Zelvarix for the accounts that matter enough to research properly. That's a reasonable setup, and Zelvarix's CSV export makes it easy.

Common questions

Is Zelvarix a direct replacement for Apollo.io?

For finding and researching prospects, yes. Zelvarix doesn't include email sequencing, a dialer, or a CRM, so if your team depends on Apollo's engagement tools you'd keep those or use a separate tool for them. Zelvarix does the front of the process: find the right contacts and give reps intelligence on each one.

How much does Apollo cost for a 5-person sales team?

On the Professional plan at $79 per user per month billed annually, five seats cost $395 a month, or $495 month-to-month. Credits for phone numbers and enrichment are consumed on top of the seat fee. Zelvarix Pro covers a team of up to five for a flat $99 a month.

Does Zelvarix have a free plan like Apollo?

Zelvarix offers a 7-day free trial with no credit card required rather than a permanent free tier. Every paid plan includes the full AI intelligence panel and plain-English search.

Where does Zelvarix get its contact data?

Zelvarix licenses contact data from People Data Labs, a B2B data provider used by many enterprise sales and recruiting platforms. Zelvarix adds the plain-English search layer and the AI intelligence on each contact on top of that data.

Can I switch from Apollo to Zelvarix without losing my lists?

Zelvarix Lists organizes saved contacts and exports them to CSV, so anything you find in Zelvarix moves into any CRM or sequencing tool. Importing your existing Apollo exports into Zelvarix isn't currently supported.
