5 prospecting tools for small sales teams, and why most of them punish you for growing

Search "prospecting tools" and you'll find a dozen lists that all say the same thing about the same five products. This one focuses on the question a 2–10 person team actually has: what does this cost once everyone on my team needs a seat, not just the cheapest single-user price on the homepage.

The pattern: almost every tool in this category charges per user. That's a fine model if you're selling to a 200-person revenue org. For a team of two to ten, it means your tool bill grows every time you hire — regardless of how much any one rep actually uses it.

2. Apollo.io — the biggest database, priced per seat

$49–$119/user/month (annual billing)

Apollo has the broadest feature set on this list: search, sequencing, a dialer, and a large free tier. For a solo user or someone who mostly needs the free plan, it's genuinely hard to beat. The catch is per-seat math: five reps on the Professional plan runs $395 a month billed annually, or $495 month-to-month, before credit usage for phone reveals and enrichment. A team that outgrows the free tier will feel the per-seat cost immediately.

3. ZoomInfo — the deepest data, priced for enterprise

Custom quote, reported ~$15,000–$60,000+/year

ZoomInfo's proprietary database and intent data are best-in-class, and for an enterprise revenue org with a procurement budget, that depth is worth the price. It's not built for small teams: pricing isn't published, contracts are annual, and additional seats cost roughly $1,500–$2,500 a year each on top of a five-figure platform fee. A five-person team is usually the smallest, least profitable account on ZoomInfo's books.

4. Lusha — lower entry price, still per seat

Reported ~$30–$50/user/month on paid plans, credit-based

Lusha's Chrome extension and lower headline price make it popular with individual reps who need fast contact lookups on LinkedIn. Worth flagging: multiple independent reviews note Lusha's own pricing page, FAQ, and vendor listings have shown conflicting numbers at different times, so treat any published figure as a starting point to confirm at signup, not a fixed rate. It's still priced per user, and features like CRM sync and bulk enrichment are commonly gated to higher tiers.

5. Seamless.ai — real-time search, pricing behind a sales call

Reported ~$147+/month and up, quote-based beyond a limited free tier

Seamless searches for contact data in real time rather than only pulling from a static database, which can surface people other tools miss — at the cost of more variable accuracy. Beyond a small free tier, Seamless doesn't publish pricing; reported figures from buyers cluster around $147 a month and climb from there, sold as annual contracts. Multiple independent reviews also flag difficulty cancelling and auto-renewal complaints as a recurring theme worth reading the contract for closely.

Most established sales intelligence platforms, including Apollo, ZoomInfo, Lusha, and Seamless.ai, charge per user, so cost rises every time you add a rep. Zelvarix uses flat team pricing instead: one price covers a set number of seats, and adding reps within that limit costs nothing extra.

Why do most sales intelligence tools charge per seat?

Per-seat pricing scales revenue with a customer's headcount, which works well for vendors selling into large sales orgs. For a team of two to ten, it means the bill grows with every hire regardless of how much the tool is actually used.
